# PRODUCT.md
## 1. The job
When the dates are confirmed and the client asks for a proposal, I want to turn the brief into a value-led, costed plan quickly, so the client sees exactly what every rupee buys and picks us.
Who, by situation: project head at a top-tier agency, with a confirmed date and a client comparing 3 agencies
Today they hire: Excel, PPT, old proposals repurposed, WhatsApps to vendors for quotes
## 2. The switch
What they'd fire: generic, assumption-heavy proposals built from Excel, PPT and old decks
Push: proposals look alike, so agencies end up competing on price
Pull: a proposal backed by real numbers from recently executed projects, showing exactly what the client's money buys
Anxiety: I can't put my name on numbers a bot made; once-in-a-lifetime event, no retake, needs a backup plan
Habit: the project head trusts his own experience and his own Excel
The one worry onboarding must remove: losing control of the numbers, so every line stays editable and signed off by the project head
How I know: a prominent industrialist replaced their agency mid-way and brought us in; we won on value and standards, not price
## 3. The core flow
The story: a project head turns fragmented client inputs into a clear, value-led, costed proposal.
1. The moment it hurts: dates are confirmed and the client wants a proposal quickly, often while comparing multiple agencies. Risk: the brief is incomplete, the deadline is tight, or key context is scattered across people and channels.
2. The project head gives the tool: WhatsApp text and PDF briefs. Principle: the client communicates naturally, the system does the organising. Risk: inputs are missed, duplicated or misunderstood.
3. The tool structures and prices: it converts the inputs into scope, assumptions, gaps and a cost build-up. In v1, each line is priced from an uploaded Excel costing sheet from a recently executed project, with the source of every number visible. Pricing is shown as a defensible range, not false precision, subject to timing of confirmation, negotiation and external factors. Where appropriate, a 5-10% contingency reserve is included. Risk: the uploaded costing is outdated, the past project is not truly comparable, market conditions have changed, or the contingency is insufficient.
4. The project head checks and changes: scope, assumptions, quantities, quality levels, vendors, costs, margins and options. Risk: a bad assumption is approved, a dependency is missed, or a manual change creates inconsistency.
5. The job is done when: the agency has a clear, credible, value-led proposal it can confidently stand behind, and the client understands what they are paying for, why it costs what it does, and what outcome that spend delivers. Risk: unresolved assumptions, unconfirmed costs or unclear deliverables remain.
Later: approved rate sheets, current vendor quotations, voice notes, email and other input formats.
## 4. Onboarding
1. The project head opens the link and sees: a simple screen asking for the client brief and one recent Excel costing sheet, with this line above the upload button: For privacy, you are free to change client names, but please keep the figures intact. Your data will not be shared without your prior consent.
2. The one thing they do first: paste or upload the brief and upload the Excel costing sheet.
3. The first value they see, in under 5 minutes: a first-pass project cost range with the major cost heads and the source of each number visible. The first screen says: Estimated project cost: Rs X to Rs Y. Built from your uploaded costing data.
Trust moment: the project head sees a number they recognise from their own recent project and can trace it straight back to the source. The reaction we want: this isn't making up numbers, it's using my numbers.
Login: asked only after the first value.
## 5. v1
Does: takes a WhatsApp text or PDF brief plus one uploaded Excel costing sheet, structures the requirement, produces a first-pass project cost range with major cost heads and the source of each number visible, and lets the project head correct the result. Offers login after the first value, only to save work.
Doesn't: require login before first value; export a finished proposal; fetch live vendor prices; connect to vendors; read voice notes or email; use approved rate-sheet libraries; manage projects end-to-end; automate negotiations, approvals or client communication. No export: no PDF, no Excel, nothing sent to anyone. Without a login nothing is stored; the proposal lasts only for the session. Saving a proposal, with its brief and source sheet, needs a login. Download (PDF and Excel together) is a later milestone.
Shown on screen: Please note: to store data, logging in is mandatory. Any data generated for testing without login will not be stored.
Day-one test: can a project head upload the brief and one costing sheet, see a believable number in under five minutes, understand where it came from, and correct it? If yes, v1 has done its job.
## 6. Riskiest guess
The guess: a project head can upload one real client brief and one recent Excel costing sheet, and the system produces a first-pass cost range by major head that is credible enough to review and refine.
How I test it (30 minutes, no code): take one real past client brief and one real costing sheet from a recently executed project. Give both to ChatGPT and ask it to structure the brief into major cost heads, map the past costs to those heads, show the source of each number, flag assumptions and missing information, and produce a defensible cost range with contingency. Then compare it with what an experienced project head would estimate manually.
Pass: the total lands within 10% of the project head's own estimate, and every cost head shows its source.
What happened: not run yet. If the costing sheet is too messy, too specific or too incomplete to support the estimate, we find out before building.
Tested 6 Oct 2026 on a real wedding brief + the 19.08 Tara sheet: tool total ₹8.41 crore pre-GST vs my own estimate ₹8.56 crore, 1.8% apart, inside the ±10% pass line.
## 7. Milestones
Sequence: read, understand, map, price, explain uncertainty, correct, trust, save.
1. I can upload one Excel costing sheet and see its cost heads and values read back correctly.
2. I can paste a WhatsApp brief or upload a PDF and see the client requirements structured into clear project needs.
3. I can see those requirements mapped to relevant cost heads from the uploaded costing sheet.
4. I can see a first-pass cost range for each major head, with the source of every number visible.
5. I can see assumptions, missing information and uncertain costs clearly flagged instead of hidden.
6. I can see an appropriate contingency applied where required and understand why it has been added.
7. I can change a quantity, assumption, cost or contingency and immediately see the revised project cost range.
8. I can review one consolidated view of scope, assumptions and costs. Deliverables and timelines stay out: the non-costing parts of the client proposal already cover them.
9. I can reach a credible first-pass costing from the brief and costing sheet in under five minutes.
10. I can create an account after seeing the first value, close the product, return later, and find my corrected costing saved exactly as I left it.
